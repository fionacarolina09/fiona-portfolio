GSTC vs ISO 21101: Understanding the Differences and Synergies

Tourism organizations exploring sustainability initiatives often encounter two frameworks: the Global Sustainable Tourism Council (GSTC) Criteria and ISO 21101:2014 Adventure Tourism — Safety Management Systems. While they are sometimes discussed separately, both frameworks can complement each other when implemented strategically. Understanding their purpose, scope and areas of focus helps organizations make informed decisions based on their business objectives.

Key Takeaways

  • GSTC focuses primarily on tourism sustainability.
  • ISO 21101 focuses primarily on adventure tourism safety management.
  • Both frameworks promote leadership, planning, monitoring and continual improvement.
  • GSTC and ISO 21101 are not competing frameworks.
  • Organizations can benefit from implementing elements of both.

Overview of GSTC and ISO 21101

Both GSTC and ISO 21101 are internationally recognized frameworks that support responsible tourism practices. However, they were developed to address different organizational objectives.

GSTC focuses on sustainability outcomes across environmental, social, cultural and economic dimensions. ISO 21101 focuses on managing safety risks associated with adventure tourism activities through a structured management system.

What Is GSTC?

The Global Sustainable Tourism Council (GSTC) establishes globally recognized criteria for sustainable tourism. The criteria are designed to support tourism businesses and destinations in managing sustainability effectively.

Sustainable Management

Governance, planning, monitoring and continual improvement.

Social & Economic Benefits

Supporting local communities and economic development.

Cultural Heritage

Protection and promotion of local culture and traditions.

Environmental Responsibility

Resource conservation and environmental protection.

What Is ISO 21101?

ISO 21101:2014 specifies requirements for a safety management system for adventure tourism activity providers. The standard helps organizations manage operational risks while delivering safe tourism experiences.

ISO 21101 Focus Area Purpose
Leadership Establish accountability and commitment.
Risk Management Identify and control activity-related risks.
Competence Ensure personnel capability.
Operational Control Deliver activities consistently and safely.
Emergency Preparedness Respond effectively to incidents.
Continual Improvement Improve system effectiveness over time.

Where Both Frameworks Align

Despite their different objectives, GSTC and ISO 21101 share several common management principles.

Leadership

Management commitment and accountability.

Planning

Objectives, responsibilities and implementation planning.

Monitoring

Performance measurement and review.

Improvement

Continual improvement and corrective actions.

Can GSTC and ISO 21101 Be Implemented Together?

Yes. Organizations delivering adventure tourism activities may benefit from integrating both frameworks.

GSTC can provide the sustainability framework, while ISO 21101 provides the operational and safety management framework.

Which Framework Is Right for Your Organization?

The answer depends on your objectives, operations and strategic priorities.

  • Choose GSTC when sustainability is a strategic priority.
  • Choose ISO 21101 when managing adventure tourism risks is critical.
  • Consider both when sustainability and safety are equally important.

Organizations do not need to treat these frameworks as alternatives. In many cases, they can work together to support responsible and resilient tourism operations.
