ISO 14001:2026 Transition Timeline: Key DSM Deadlines Explained

The Department of Standards Malaysia has issued ACB Circular 5/2026 setting out the transition requirements from ISO 14001:2015 to ISO 14001:2026.

The circular establishes a three-year transition period and defines important milestones for accredited Certification Bodies, new certifications and existing ISO 14001:2015 certified organisations.

10 min read Based on DSM ACB Circular 5/2026

Key Takeaways

  • ISO 14001:2026 was published in April 2026.
  • The transition period from ISO 14001:2015 is three years.
  • Accredited Certification Bodies must complete their transition by 30 April 2027.
  • New and initial certifications must be based on ISO 14001:2026 after 31 October 2027.
  • Existing certified organisations must complete their transition by 30 April 2029.
  • After 30 April 2029, accredited ISO 14001:2015 certification will no longer be accepted.

What Is DSM ACB Circular 5/2026?

ACB Circular 5/2026 was issued by the Department of Standards Malaysia on 29 June 2026. It establishes the transition requirements for ISO 14001:2026, which replaces ISO 14001:2015.

The circular is primarily directed to applicant and accredited Certification Bodies operating under the Standards Malaysia Accreditation of Certification Bodies Scheme.

However, the dates in the circular are also important to organisations that currently hold ISO 14001:2015 certification or are planning to obtain environmental management system certification.

Important distinction

Some dates in the circular apply directly to Certification Bodies rather than certified organisations. Organisations should nevertheless understand these milestones because they affect the availability and issuance of accredited ISO 14001:2026 certification.

ISO 14001:2026 Transition Timeline

Date Transition Milestone Primary Application

30 April 2029 Certification Bodies must complete the transition of all existing certified clients. Existing ISO 14001:2015 certified organisations
After 30 April 2029 Accredited certification to ISO 14001:2015 will no longer be accepted. All existing certified organisations

What Each Transition Milestone Means

April 2026: Revised Standard Published

ISO 14001:2026 was published in April 2026 and formally replaced ISO 14001:2015 as the current edition of the environmental management system standard.

29 June 2026: DSM Circular Issued

DSM issued ACB Circular 5/2026 to establish the national accreditation transition requirements and related deadlines.

31 January 2027: Transition Declaration

Accredited Certification Bodies must submit a formal declaration and supporting evidence confirming their readiness to transition to the revised standard.

30 April 2027: Certification Body Transition

Accredited Certification Bodies must complete their transition to ISO 14001:2026 by this date before issuing accredited certificates to the revised standard.

31 October 2027: New Certifications

After this date, new and initial ISO 14001 certifications must be conducted against ISO 14001:2026 rather than ISO 14001:2015.

30 April 2029: Final Client Deadline

All existing certified clients must complete their transition before the end of the three-year transition period.

What Existing ISO 14001:2015 Certified Organisations Should Do

Organisations currently certified to ISO 14001:2015 should not treat 30 April 2029 as the date to begin transition activities. It is the final date by which the certification transition must already be completed.

Adequate time should be allowed to understand the revised requirements, update the environmental management system, implement the necessary changes and verify that the revised arrangements are effective.

  • Obtain and review ISO 14001:2026.
  • Conduct a structured transition gap assessment.
  • Identify affected EMS processes and documented information.
  • Assign responsibilities and establish a transition plan.
  • Provide awareness or transition training to relevant personnel.
  • Implement and retain evidence of the revised arrangements.
  • Conduct an internal audit against ISO 14001:2026.
  • Review transition readiness through management review.
  • Coordinate the transition audit with the Certification Body.

The transition deadline relates to successful certification transition, not merely completion of a gap assessment or revision of EMS documents. Organisations should ensure that revised requirements are implemented and supported by objective evidence before the transition audit.

What About Organisations Seeking Initial Certification?

Organisations planning to obtain ISO 14001 certification should consider the timing of their implementation and certification programme.

Under the DSM transition timeline, new and initial certifications issued after 31 October 2027 must be based on ISO 14001:2026.

An organisation beginning its EMS implementation during the transition period should therefore assess whether it is practical to implement ISO 14001:2026 directly rather than establishing a system against the previous edition and transitioning shortly afterwards.

Practical consideration

Organisations planning initial certification in late 2027 or afterwards should build their environmental management system against ISO 14001:2026 from the beginning.

Recommended ISO 14001:2026 Transition Roadmap

Phase 1: Understand the Revised Requirements

  • Obtain the revised standard.
  • Identify new, amended and clarified requirements.
  • Brief Top Management and relevant process owners.

Phase 2: Conduct a Gap Assessment

  • Compare the existing EMS against ISO 14001:2026.
  • Identify implementation, competency and documentation gaps.
  • Prioritise actions based on environmental and certification risks.

Phase 3: Establish the Transition Plan

  • Define actions, responsibilities and completion dates.
  • Align transition activities with the surveillance or recertification cycle.
  • Allocate sufficient resources for implementation and verification.

Phase 4: Update and Implement the EMS

  • Revise affected processes and documented information.
  • Communicate changes to relevant personnel and external providers.
  • Retain evidence demonstrating implementation.

Phase 5: Verify Transition Readiness

  • Conduct an internal audit against ISO 14001:2026.
  • Address identified nonconformities and implementation weaknesses.
  • Review EMS performance and transition status during management review.

Phase 6: Complete Certification Transition

  • Confirm transition arrangements with the Certification Body.
  • Complete the required transition audit.
  • Resolve any findings within the agreed timeframe.
  • Obtain the upgraded ISO 14001:2026 certificate before 30 April 2029.

Suggested Planning Timeline for Certified Organisations

The following is a practical planning approach rather than a mandatory schedule stated in the DSM circular.

Suggested Period Recommended Activities
2026 Review ISO 14001:2026, conduct awareness training and begin the transition gap assessment.
Early to Mid-2027 Establish the transition plan, update affected processes and revise documented information.
Late 2027 to 2028 Complete implementation, build operational evidence and conduct internal audits against ISO 14001:2026.
During 2028 Complete management review and arrange the transition audit through the normal certification cycle where practicable.
Before 30 April 2029 Close audit findings and obtain the upgraded ISO 14001:2026 certification.

Common ISO 14001 Transition Mistakes to Avoid

Waiting Until the Final Year

Leaving the transition until 2029 may create insufficient time to implement changes, complete internal verification and resolve certification findings.

Treating Transition as Documentation Only

Revised documents alone do not demonstrate effective implementation. Auditors will normally seek objective evidence that updated processes are operating.

Insufficient Competence Development

EMS coordinators, process owners, internal auditors and relevant personnel should understand the changes affecting their responsibilities.

Not Aligning With the Audit Cycle

Transition planning should consider the organisation’s surveillance and recertification schedule to avoid unnecessary time pressure.

Using an Outdated Audit Checklist

Internal audit criteria and checklists should be updated before the organisation evaluates its readiness for transition.

Missing Management Review Evidence

Top Management should review transition progress, EMS changes, resource needs and readiness before the external transition audit.
