ISO 45001 Revision Reaches DIS Stage: Key Proposed Changes to Watch

The revision of ISO 45001 has reached the Draft International Standard (DIS) stage, providing the clearest indication so far of the direction of the next edition of the occupational health and safety management system standard.

ISO/DIS 45001 introduces proposed changes affecting work-related well-being, psychosocial risks, occupational health, return-to-work arrangements, external providers, operational controls, and other areas of the OH&S management system.

ISO/DIS 45001 2026 Revision Update OH&S Management Systems

Important Disclaimer: ISO/DIS 45001 is a Draft International Standard and is not the final published edition of ISO 45001. The requirements, terminology, clause structure, and other provisions discussed in this article may change during the remaining ISO development process.

Organizations should not treat the proposed changes as formal certification or transition requirements at this stage. ISO 45001:2018 remains the current published certification standard until the revised edition is formally issued.

Key Takeaways

  • ISO 45001 has progressed to the Draft International Standard (DIS) stage.
  • The DIS represents a proposed revision and does not yet contain final requirements.
  • Work-related well-being and psychosocial risks receive greater attention in the draft.
  • New dedicated provisions are proposed for occupational health and return to work following injury and ill health.
  • Requirements relating to externally provided processes, products, and services are proposed to be restructured.
  • Annex A guidance is expected to become substantially more detailed.
  • Organizations should monitor and understand the changes, but formal transition activities should wait for the final published standard.

Current Status of the ISO 45001 Revision

ISO 45001:2018 is undergoing its first major revision since publication. In 2026, the revision progressed to the Draft International Standard stage as ISO/DIS 45001.

The DIS provides organizations, occupational health and safety professionals, auditors, consultants, and other interested parties with an advanced view of the proposed direction of the revised standard.

Item Current Position
Current published standard ISO 45001:2018
Climate amendment ISO 45001:2018/Amd 1:2024
Revision under development ISO/DIS 45001 — Edition 2
Current development stage Draft International Standard (DIS)
Certification requirement ISO 45001:2018 remains applicable
Expected final publication Currently anticipated in 2027, subject to the ISO development process

What Does the DIS Stage Mean?

DIS stands for Draft International Standard. It is an advanced stage in the ISO standards development process where the proposed technical requirements have developed sufficiently for wider review and voting by ISO national member bodies.

Reaching the DIS stage is significant because it provides a much clearer indication of the likely direction of the revised standard. However, the content is still subject to comments, technical review, revision, and further approval.

DIS does not mean final. Requirements appearing in ISO/DIS 45001 may be retained, modified, reorganized, clarified, or removed before the revised International Standard is formally published.

Key Proposed Changes in ISO/DIS 45001

The overall revision appears moderate, but several areas could have meaningful implications for organizations implementing and auditing occupational health and safety management systems.

Work-Related Well-Being

  • Greater visibility throughout the OH&S management system
  • New terminology relating to work-related well-being
  • Broader consideration of conditions affecting workers
  • Stronger relationship between health, safety, and well-being

Psychosocial Risks

  • Greater attention to psychosocial hazards
  • Work organization and workload considerations
  • Fatigue and occupational stress factors
  • Violence, bullying, and other psychosocial conditions

Occupational Health

  • Proposed dedicated Clause 8.1.3
  • More explicit occupational health controls
  • Integration of health protection into operational controls
  • Greater visibility of occupational health arrangements

Return to Work

  • Proposed dedicated Clause 8.1.4
  • Return following injury or ill health
  • Controlled follow-up arrangements
  • Greater integration with occupational health processes

External Providers

  • Restructured external provider controls
  • Externally provided processes, products, and services
  • Revised procurement-related arrangements
  • Potential implications for contractor and supplier controls

Updated Management System Structure

  • Alignment with the latest ISO Harmonized Structure
  • Updated management system terminology
  • Reorganization of selected requirements
  • Improved alignment with other ISO management system standards

Major Proposed Changes to Clause 8

Clause 8 — Operational planning and control — appears to contain some of the most significant practical changes within ISO/DIS 45001.

The proposed changes could affect how organizations establish, implement, control, and demonstrate operational arrangements relating to occupational health and safety.

Proposed Area Potential Significance
8.1.1 General Expanded operational planning and control provisions.
8.1.2 Eliminating hazards and reducing OH&S risks Greater detail relating to hazard elimination and risk reduction controls.
8.1.3 Occupational health New dedicated provisions addressing occupational health processes.
8.1.4 Return to work following injury and ill health New requirements relating to controlled return-to-work arrangements.
Management of change Continued emphasis on controlling OH&S implications arising from change.
8.1.6 Externally provided processes, products and services Restructured controls for externally provided activities and resources.

The clause numbers and wording above reflect the Draft International Standard and should not yet be used as final certification criteria.

Changes to Terms and Definitions

ISO/DIS 45001 also proposes several changes to terminology that could influence how the future requirements are interpreted and implemented.

Work-Related Well-Being

A new concept intended to strengthen consideration of worker well-being within the OH&S management system.

Disability

New terminology supporting broader consideration of workers and workplace conditions.

External Provider

Terminology supporting the proposed restructuring of controls for externally provided processes, products, and services.

OH&S Risk

The definition of occupational health and safety risk is proposed to be revised as part of the updated terminology.

Expanded Annex A Guidance

Annex A is expected to become more significant in the revised edition. The DIS contains substantially expanded guidance intended to support clearer interpretation and application of the requirements.

This could be particularly valuable for organizations and professionals seeking greater clarity on the intended application of specific clauses.

  • OH&S practitioners
  • Internal auditors
  • Certification auditors
  • Management system consultants
  • OH&S managers and coordinators
  • Organizations implementing integrated management systems

Once the final standard is published, Annex A should therefore be reviewed alongside the normative requirements rather than treated merely as supplementary reading.

What Should Organizations Do Now?

Organizations do not need to begin formal transition activities at the DIS stage. However, monitoring the proposed changes can help identify potential areas requiring future attention.

1. Monitor the Revision

Follow further ISO announcements and subsequent development stages before making formal system changes.

2. Review Psychosocial Risks

Evaluate whether existing hazard identification processes adequately consider psychosocial hazards and contemporary working conditions.

3. Review Occupational Health

Examine existing occupational health arrangements, health surveillance, exposure controls, and related processes.

4. Review Return-to-Work Processes

Determine whether arrangements following injury or ill health are defined, controlled, and appropriately implemented.

5. Review External Provider Controls

Evaluate how contractors, suppliers, outsourced activities, and other external providers are controlled from an OH&S perspective.

6. Maintain ISO 45001:2018 Compliance

Continue implementing, auditing, and maintaining the OH&S management system against the currently published standard.

Recommended approach: Monitor, understand, and assess readiness — but do not redesign the OH&S management system solely around draft requirements that may still change.

When Will the Revised ISO 45001 Be Published?

Current indications point towards publication of the revised ISO 45001 in 2027. However, the final publication date remains dependent on the ISO standards development process and resolution of comments arising from the draft stages.

For this reason, referring to the revision as ISO 45001:2026 or definitively as ISO 45001:2027 may be premature until ISO formally publishes and designates the new edition.

The most accurate terminology at this stage is ISO/DIS 45001 or the ISO 45001 revision.

What Happens After the DIS Stage?

Comments and votes received during the DIS process must be reviewed and resolved through the ISO standards development process.

As a result, provisions currently appearing in ISO/DIS 45001 may be:

  • retained;
  • revised;
  • clarified;
  • reorganized; or
  • removed before final publication.

Organizations should therefore continue monitoring the development of the standard rather than treating the current DIS as the final transition specification.

Conclusion

ISO/DIS 45001 provides the clearest indication so far of how the next edition of ISO 45001 may develop.

The proposed revision reflects a broader approach to occupational health and safety, with increased attention to work-related well-being, psychosocial risks, occupational health, return to work, operational controls, and externally provided processes, products, and services.

Organizations with mature ISO 45001 management systems may find many of the concepts familiar, although several proposed Clause 8 changes could require more focused review once the final standard is issued.

For now, the appropriate approach is to monitor, understand, and prepare — not formally transition.

ISO 45001:2018 remains the current published certification standard, and ISO/DIS 45001 should continue to be treated as a draft until the revised International Standard is formally published.
