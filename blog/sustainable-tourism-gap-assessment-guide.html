Sustainable Tourism

How to Conduct a Sustainable Tourism Gap Assessment

Many tourism organizations want to improve their sustainability performance but struggle to determine where to start. A sustainable tourism gap assessment provides a structured method for evaluating current practices, identifying improvement opportunities and establishing priorities for implementation. Whether your organization is exploring GSTC-aligned practices or strengthening existing sustainability initiatives, a gap assessment is often the most effective first step.

Key Takeaways

  • A gap assessment identifies strengths, weaknesses and improvement opportunities.
  • Organizations can assess sustainability without pursuing certification.
  • A structured methodology helps prioritize resources effectively.
  • Management system principles support objective and evidence-based assessments.
  • Gap assessments provide a foundation for sustainability action planning.

What Is a Sustainable Tourism Gap Assessment?

A gap assessment is a structured review of an organization's current sustainability practices against defined expectations, objectives or sustainability frameworks.

The purpose is not to determine compliance or nonconformity. Instead, it helps organizations understand their current level of maturity and identify areas requiring improvement.

For tourism businesses, a gap assessment can provide valuable insight into sustainability governance, stakeholder engagement, environmental management, cultural heritage protection and community contributions.

Why Conduct a Gap Assessment?

Many organizations invest significant resources into sustainability initiatives without first understanding their current position.

Without a Gap Assessment With a Gap Assessment
Assumptions drive decisions Evidence drives decisions
Resources may be misallocated Improvement priorities become clearer
Limited visibility of weaknesses Strengths and weaknesses identified
Reactive improvements Planned improvements

Key Areas to Assess

Sustainable tourism assessments typically focus on four broad areas.

Sustainable Management

Governance, planning, objectives, stakeholder engagement and performance monitoring.

Social & Economic Benefits

Local employment, community engagement and local procurement practices.

Cultural Heritage

Protection, promotion and respectful representation of local culture.

Environmental Responsibility

Energy, water, waste, pollution prevention and biodiversity considerations.

A Practical Gap Assessment Methodology

A structured approach improves consistency and reliability.

Step Activity
1 Define assessment objectives and scope.
2 Identify assessment criteria.
3 Review existing practices and documentation.
4 Interview relevant personnel.
5 Evaluate evidence.
6 Identify strengths and gaps.
7 Prioritize improvement actions.

Gathering Objective Evidence

Effective assessments rely on objective evidence rather than assumptions.

Examples include:

  • Sustainability objectives and plans.
  • Training records.
  • Customer feedback data.
  • Community engagement records.
  • Environmental performance data.
  • Management review outputs.
  • Operational procedures and guidelines.

Using objective evidence helps ensure findings are reliable and actionable.

Common Sustainability Gaps Identified

Area Common Gap
Governance No defined sustainability responsibilities.
Planning No measurable sustainability objectives.
Community Limited engagement with local stakeholders.
Culture No formal consideration of cultural heritage.
Environment Performance data not monitored.
Improvement Actions implemented but not reviewed.

What Happens After the Assessment?

The value of a gap assessment lies in what happens next.

Assessment findings should be translated into measurable objectives, action plans, responsibilities and performance indicators.

Organizations should review progress periodically and continually improve sustainability performance over time.
